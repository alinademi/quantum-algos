<Depth level="kid">

Imagine two cards inside a special box. A helper reports only whether the cards match, without showing either card. If the answer is yes, you cannot tell whether both cards show zero or both show one. In a quantum experiment that distinction is more than missing knowledge: the two possibilities can still contribute together to a wave. They can still cancel or reinforce in a later experiment. Reading both cards would give too much information and ruin that ability. The picture breaks because ordinary hidden cards already have definite faces; the quantum description uses amplitudes for the possible paired results.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

Prepare a helper in |0⟩. Apply [CNOT](/learn/cnot/) from each data wire into the helper. Its value on each basis input is the XOR of the two data bits. [Measure just the helper](/learn/partial-measurement/): 0 means even parity (00 or 11), and 1 means odd (01 or 10). With both data qubits in |+⟩, the even result leaves equal amplitudes for 00 and 11; the odd result leaves equal amplitudes for 01 and 10. Each is a Bell state. The measurement records no distinction within the chosen parity, so coherence survives. If one bit was already known, parity can also reveal the other. Both methods give the same parity odds, but try the separate XX test: from |++⟩, either helper result has XX expectation +1, whereas measuring both Z values first gives 0.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

The observable is $Z\otimes Z$, with projectors $P_+=(I+ZZ)/2$ and $P_-=(I-ZZ)/2$. The two CNOTs map $|a,b,0\rangle$ to $|a,b,a\oplus b\rangle$. Helper outcome 0 has eigenvalue +1; 1 has eigenvalue −1. The post-measurement state preserves superpositions within the corresponding subspace. Measuring both data qubits separately is a finer measurement. For $|++\rangle$, each outcome of the parity-only measurement yields a Bell state with XX expectation +1. Recording both Z values instead makes each parity-conditioned data state diagonal in Z, with XX expectation 0. The lab displays this alternative observable, not a simultaneous measurement of XX and the individual Z values.

</Depth>
