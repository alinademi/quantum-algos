<Depth level="kid">

Imagine exchanging special particles along paths. The order of exchanges can act on information stored collectively, even if you never read the individual particles.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

The lab models a fusion qubit of Ising anyons. Two adjacent braid generators act like 90-degree Z and X rotations, up to a common overall phase. Exchanging in different orders gives different matrices. Three-exchange braid relations still hold.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

In a conventional four-Ising-anyon fusion basis with fixed total charge, adjacent braid generators can be represented (with common phase removed) by $B_1=R_Z(\pi/2)$ and $B_2=R_X(\pi/2)$, for one handedness and basis convention. They obey $B_1 B_2 B_1=B_2 B_1 B_2$ but do not commute. Topological protection is model- and implementation-dependent; Ising braids generate Clifford operations and need additional resources for universality. This is an ideal fusion-space matrix model, not a simulation of moving physical particles.

</Depth>
