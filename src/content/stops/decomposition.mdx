<Depth level="kid">

Take a complicated transformation and undo small parts until only easy phases are left. Reverse those small steps to reconstruct the original operation.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

A two-level unitary acts only on two computational basis amplitudes. You can use such rotations to eliminate entries below a matrix diagonal, like Gaussian elimination but keeping the operation unitary. Reverse the eliminations to reconstruct the original.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

Complex Givens rotations mix rows of a unitary U to produce an upper-triangular unitary, necessarily diagonal. If $G_m\cdots G_1 U=D$, then $U=G_1^\dagger\cdots G_m^\dagger D$. A dimension-d matrix needs at most $d(d-1)/2$ such eliminations. The lab decomposes 4 by 4 matrices into two-level factors; each factor is still abstract and may itself require elementary gates. A Gray-code path connects any two basis strings by single-bit changes. Conjugate the two-level operation by basis-state transpositions along that path until the two strings differ at just one bit, then use a multi-controlled one-qubit gate and undo the transpositions. This explains how two-level factors reduce to smaller gates.

</Depth>
