<Depth level="kid">

Some huge lists of quantum numbers can be replaced by much smaller rules. With the right gates you can update the rules directly, even for many entangled wires.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

Start with generators that describe all-zero qubits, conjugate them by Clifford gates, and compute Pauli measurement probabilities from the resulting group. If a signed Pauli observable belongs to the stabilizer group its outcome is fixed; otherwise the two outcomes are equally likely.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

Represent each signed Pauli by binary X and Z components and phase information. Clifford conjugation updates these in polynomial time. For a pure stabilizer state, a Hermitian Pauli Q has expectation +1 if Q is in S, −1 if −Q is in S, and 0 otherwise. The lab uses binary elimination to test membership with signs, not a dense vector. Full stabilizer simulation also updates the generators after measurements; the lab computes probabilities before a single chosen measurement. See [Aaronson and Gottesman](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0406196).

</Depth>
