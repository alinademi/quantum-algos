<Depth level="kid">

Imagine a locked box with two keys. It flips a token only if both keys are turned. The two keys are still there afterward, and turning the box mechanism again restores the token. Start the token at zero and it stores whether both keys were on.

There is another useful reversible trick. To exchange two switches without a spare switch, flip the second according to the first, the first according to the second, then the second according to the first. Their values have swapped. These pictures break because quantum switches can carry complex amplitudes; the real gates act on their joint state without reading the switches.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

Toffoli (CCNOT) maps three bits (a,b,t) to (a,b,t XOR (a AND b)). A target started at 0 stores the AND while the inputs remain. With an arbitrary target it still works reversibly; repeating Toffoli undoes the change.

SWAP exchanges two qubits. Three [CNOTs](/learn/cnot/) make it: first control a onto b, then b onto a, then a onto b. For example 10 becomes 11, then 01, then stays 01. The same maps hold on amplitudes by linearity, not just on definite bits. The third wire in the SWAP example remains untouched. Neither operation measures its inputs.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

Toffoli acts as $|a,b,t\rangle\mapsto |a,b,t\oplus ab\rangle$. It is a self-inverse permutation matrix. With target 0 it embeds AND; with target 1 it embeds NAND. It is universal for reversible classical computation with suitable extra bits, not for arbitrary quantum unitaries on its own. Together with Hadamard, it can approximate universal quantum computation using a real-amplitude encoding with an extra qubit ([Aharonov 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0301040)).

The SWAP decomposition on two wires is
$(a,b)\to(a,a\oplus b)\to(b,a\oplus b)\to(b,a)$,
so $CX_{01}CX_{10}CX_{01}=SWAP$. These identities on all basis inputs establish equality of linear operators, including on entangled inputs. SWAP sends $|a,b\rangle$ to $|b,a\rangle$ and is its own inverse.

</Depth>
