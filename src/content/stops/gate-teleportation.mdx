<Depth level="kid">

Imagine a machine that struggles to perform one special instruction on your data. Instead, prepare a special helper ahead of time, connect it to the data and read the helper's answer. That answer tells you whether the desired change happened or whether an easier correction is needed. Either way, with the right correction the data receives the intended change. The expensive helper can be prepared and checked away from the main calculation, but it is used up by the process. The picture breaks because the helper is a quantum state whose amplitudes matter, not a classical instruction card, and its measured outcome is genuinely random.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

In many error-correcting architectures, [Clifford gates](/learn/clifford-group/) are relatively cheap while T is expensive. Prepare the magic state T|+⟩ offline, often purifying noisy copies by magic-state distillation. Apply CNOT from the data to this helper and measure the helper. Outcome 0 leaves T on the data; outcome 1 leaves T† up to global phase, so apply S to correct it. Both occur with probability 1/2. This is a form of [teleportation](/learn/teleportation/), transferring a desired operation using a resource state and classical feed-forward. It supplies a non-Clifford operation for [universality](/learn/universal/), but does not remove the cost of preparing the resource.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

Write $|\psi\rangle=a|0\rangle+b|1\rangle$ and $\omega=e^{i\pi/4}$. After CNOT, projecting the magic qubit onto 0 gives $(a|0\rangle+\omega b|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$; outcome 1 gives $(\omega a|0\rangle+b|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$. Each has probability 1/2. Since $ST^\dagger=T$, feed-forward yields the target T gate in both branches, up to phase. The resource state is not created by Clifford gates alone. In architectures where logical Clifford operations are relatively cheap, T injection moves the non-Clifford cost into offline preparation and magic-state distillation, as in [Bravyi and Kitaev](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0403025). See [Gottesman and Chuang](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9908010).

</Depth>
