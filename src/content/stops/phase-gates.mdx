answer: "A phase gate changes the relative phase between |0⟩ and |1⟩ without changing their immediate Z-basis probabilities."
myth:
  claim: "A phase gate does nothing because the measurement odds stay the same."
  truth: "Relative phase can change the probabilities after a later gate such as H."
---

<Depth level="kid">

Picture two waves that have the same heights. One hits the shore a little later than the other. Each wave is just as strong as before. If you measure the height of each on its own, you cannot tell that you delayed it. Bring the two together and the timing changes how much they reinforce or cancel.

A quantum phase operation does something similar to the two amplitudes of an ideal qubit. It can change their relative timing without changing the odds of reading 0 or 1. Choose a full, half or smaller turn and then combine the amplitudes. The picture breaks because the amplitudes are complex numbers, not separate physical waves.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

Z, S and T change the [relative phase](/learn/phase/) of the 1 amplitude by 180°, 90° and 45°. The more general P(φ) changes it by any chosen angle φ. These gates do not change either amplitude's size, so the 0/1 measurement odds stay the same.

Start in |+⟩, with equal amplitudes for 0 and 1. After Z the state is |−⟩, with the same 0/1 odds. Apply a [Hadamard](/learn/hadamard/) then measure: the original gives 0, whereas the phase-changed state gives 1. For |+⟩, angles strictly between 0° and 180° give intermediate odds. If the qubit started in 0 or 1 alone, the phase is only global and this test cannot see it.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

The phase gate is $P(\phi)=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{i\phi})$. $Z=P(\pi)$, $S=P(\pi/2)$, $T=P(\pi/4)$. $P(\phi)=e^{i\phi/2}R_Z(\phi)$. On an arbitrary pure input $(a,b)^T$, the output is $(a,e^{i\phi}b)^T$. Since the phase factor has magnitude 1, the probabilities $|a|^2$ and $|b|^2$ are unchanged. On $|+\rangle$, measuring after $HP(\phi)$ gives $p(0)=\cos^2(\phi/2)$ and $p(1)=\sin^2(\phi/2)$. The phase relating P and RZ is global for an unconditional gate but can matter when the operation is controlled.

</Depth>
