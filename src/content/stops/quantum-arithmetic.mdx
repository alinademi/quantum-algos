<Depth level="kid">

A quantum calculator can keep one number and add it to another. It can also write yes or no into a flag while leaving both original numbers available.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

For two-bit numbers A and B, use a third accumulator bit for the carry. Controlled flips update higher bits before changing the low bits needed by each carry. The comparison example XORs a flag with A < B.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

Reversible addition can implement $|a,b\rangle\mapsto|a,(b+a)\bmod 2^m\rangle$, whose inverse subtracts a. Here the accumulator is three bits, so two-bit A plus B never overflows it. The five-wire comparison is $|a,b,t\rangle\mapsto|a,b,t\oplus[a<b]\rangle$. The comparator XORs three mutually exclusive conditions: high bits 0,1; high bits both 0 and low bits 0,1; high bits both 1 and low bits 0,1. X gates turn control-on-zero into control-on-one. Both operations extend linearly to superpositions. Larger arithmetic circuits include ripple-carry adders such as [Cuccaro et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0410184), which uses a carry propagated and uncomputed, and QFT-based adders, which encode addition as phases in a Fourier basis. The multi-controlled gates here are logical gates; the preceding lesson describes their decompositions.

</Depth>
