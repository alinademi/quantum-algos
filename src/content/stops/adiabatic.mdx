<Depth level="kid">

Imagine guiding a ball along a moving lowest point. Move slowly enough and it can stay near the lowest point. A quantum ground state can similarly follow a changing energy operator under suitable conditions.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

This example starts with the ground state of −X and changes the Hamiltonian toward −Z. The desired state moves from |+⟩ toward |0⟩. The lab numerically evolves the state, rather than assuming it instantly follows the ground state.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

With $\hbar=1$, $i\partial_t|\psi\rangle=H(t)|\psi\rangle$ and $H(s)=-(1-s)X-sZ$, where $s=t/T$. Its gap is $2\sqrt{(1-s)^2+s^2}$, never zero. The lab approximates time-ordered evolution by 1000 midpoint constant-H exponentials. Under standard smoothness and nonvanishing-gap assumptions the adiabatic theorem controls error as T grows; accuracy need not improve monotonically at every finite T. Under suitable models and polynomial resource bounds, adiabatic computation and circuit computation are polynomially equivalent ([Aharonov et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0405098)). This one-qubit example is not a proof of an optimization speedup.

</Depth>
