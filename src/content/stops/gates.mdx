<Depth level="kid">

Think of two waves that can add or cancel. A gate changes how the waves fit together without losing their total strength. Another gate can undo the first.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

A one-qubit gate multiplies the two amplitudes by a 2 by 2 complex matrix. X exchanges the amplitudes, H mixes them, and Z changes one sign. Apply a gate and then its inverse in the lab: both amplitudes return. Measurement is a different operation.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

A gate on $n$ qubits is a $2^n$ by $2^n$ matrix $U$ satisfying $U^\dagger U=I$. This preserves inner products and hence normalization. The inverse is $U^\dagger$. For a circuit applying $U$ then $V$, the resulting matrix is $VU$. A general physical operation can be a channel rather than a unitary, but the ideal gates here are unitaries.

</Depth>
