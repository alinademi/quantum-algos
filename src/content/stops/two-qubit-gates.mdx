<Depth level="kid">

Imagine two musical instruments coupled by a string. You can exchange their notes, partially share their waves, or change how their waves line up without exchanging the notes. Different machines make different interactions easier: one can exchange states directly, another can make the timing of two states depend on their combination.

Ideal quantum gates describe a family of these interactions. Some exchange the full states, some exchange only part of an amplitude, and some change joint phases. The picture breaks because the two instruments have ordinary waves. Quantum amplitudes describe the joint measurements of two qubits, and the interactions can make states that cannot be described separately.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

There are several two-qubit gate families. iSWAP exchanges 01 and 10 and attaches i. √SWAP is a partial exchange that squares to SWAP. RXX and RZZ are interaction rotations; RZZ changes phases and can entangle a |++⟩ input. fSim can put 01 into a superposition of 01 and 10, and adds a phase to 11. Here MS(θ) uses the RXX(θ) convention, with the usual maximally entangling MS at 90°. ECR is an echoed cross-resonance entangler: on 00 it gives a product state, but on |+⟩|0⟩ it creates entanglement.

Different hardware makes different interactions natural: trapped ions use MS-like gates, cross-resonance superconducting devices use ECR, and tunable couplers support exchange and fSim-like operations. The lab models ideal matrices, not actual pulse errors.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

In basis order 00, 01, 10, 11, iSWAP sends 01 to $i|10\rangle$ and 10 to $i|01\rangle$. For √SWAP the symmetric subspace has eigenvalue 1 and antisymmetric eigenvalue i; squaring gives SWAP. $R_{PP}(\theta)=e^{-i\theta P\otimes P/2}$, with $P=X,Z$. Here MS(θ)=RXX(θ); physical phase conventions vary.

The fSim odd-parity block has rows $(\cos\theta,-i\sin\theta)$ and $(-i\sin\theta,\cos\theta)$, with diagonal factors 1 on 00 and $e^{-i\phi}$ on 11. ECR is $(XI-YX)/\sqrt2$ in this q0-left convention. It uses IBM’s [matrix convention](https://quantum.cloud.ibm.com/docs/en/api/qiskit/qiskit.circuit.library.ECRGate), reordered to q0-first. See [Krantz et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06560), section IV, for exchange and cross-resonance interactions. MS is used in trapped-ion systems; exact native implementations depend on hardware. Not every input is entangled by each gate: RZZ on 00 adds only a global phase, whereas on |++⟩ at 90° its concurrence is 1.

Unlike a block-diagonal [controlled gate](/learn/controlled-gates/), the exchange families act within the odd-parity subspace. Their relation to [SWAP](/learn/toffoli/) is visible on the basis inputs 01 and 10.

</Depth>
