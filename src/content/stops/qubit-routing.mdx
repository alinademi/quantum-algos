<Depth level="kid">

Suppose wires form a line, and only neighbors can talk. Move one wire’s quantum state closer to the other by swapping it with neighbors.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

On a four-site line, a CNOT between 0 and 3 needs a route. Move the control toward the target using two SWAPs, perform CNOT, then reverse the swaps. A SWAP can be expressed using three CNOTs assuming both directions are available.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

The coupling graph defines legal multi-qubit interactions; a directed graph may also constrain CNOT direction. A route changes the permutation of logical states across physical sites. For endpoints at distance d on an undirected line, the displayed restore-layout method uses $2(d-1)$ SWAPs plus one neighboring CNOT. Retaining the updated layout or using bridge circuits may reduce cost.

</Depth>
