<Depth level="kid">

Imagine a switch that decides whether a machine changes a second object. You can arrange it so the machine runs when the switch is on, or when it is off. The change might flip a token, turn an arrow or change the timing of a wave. The switch chooses which rule is applied, but the machine never reads it aloud.

In the quantum version the switch itself has amplitudes, and the rule changes the appropriate contributions to the joint state without recording a result. Even complicated controlled changes can be assembled from simpler connections and local turns. The picture breaks because the switch does not have a hidden classical value, and a change controlled this way can also change the relative phase of the switch.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

A controlled gate applies a chosen operation to its target when the control is 1, and does nothing on the other branch. CZ negates the |11⟩ amplitude. CY applies Y, which exchanges target amplitudes and adds factors of i or −i. Controlled phase CP(φ) multiplies |11⟩ by a phase e^(iφ).

Build CZ by H on the target, CNOT and H. Build CY by S†, CNOT and S on the target. For CP use half-angle phase gates and two CNOTs. To activate on 0, surround the ordinary controlled gate with X on its control. The lab compares complete matrices. Try |+⟩ on both wires: CX followed by H on the control gives 0, but C(−X) followed by H gives 1 when you measure the control.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

For control-on-one, $C(U)=|0\rangle\langle0|\otimes I+|1\rangle\langle1|\otimes U$. Control-on-zero conjugates by X on the control. $CZ=(I\otimes H)CX(I\otimes H)$ and $CY=(I\otimes S)CX(I\otimes S^\dagger)$. For CP, apply $P(\phi/2)$ to each wire, CNOT, $P(-\phi/2)$ on target, then CNOT. Comparing complete matrices checks the control phase as well.

For general $U=e^{i\gamma}V$ with $V\in SU(2)$, choose single-qubit A, B, C from the [Euler rotations](/learn/rotations/) so $ABC=I$, $AXBXC=V$. Apply C, [CNOT](/learn/cnot/), B, CNOT, A to the target, and the [phase gate](/learn/phase-gates/) $P(\gamma)$ on the control. This gives I when the control is 0 and U when it is 1.

</Depth>
