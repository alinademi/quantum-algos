<Depth level="kid">

Two-qubit gates can be complex, but there is a surprisingly small maximum number of CNOTs needed when arbitrary one-qubit gates are allowed. Some gates need fewer.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

A SWAP can be made with three CNOTs. A ZZ rotation can be made with CNOT, a Z rotation, then CNOT. A gate made only of independent one-qubit operations needs zero CNOTs.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

Up to local unitaries and phase, a two-qubit unitary has Cartan form $e^{-i(aXX+bYY+cZZ)}$. The three interaction terms commute. A general synthesis needs at most three CNOTs with arbitrary one-qubit gates, and some gates require three ([Vidal and Dawson](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0307177)). In this convention SWAP is the canonical point $(\pi/4,\pi/4,\pi/4)$ times a global phase. The lab uses selected exact examples rather than solving the general KAK factorization.

</Depth>
