<Depth level="kid">

The Clifford tool kit is limited. Adding a small new phase turn lets you build much more general transformations. Some repeated turns, however, add up to old ones.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

T is the 45-degree phase gate. Two T gates equal S, four equal Z, and eight equal identity. T† reverses T. Count T and T† separately from the other gates when estimating a circuit resource.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

T is $\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{i\pi/4})$ and is non-Clifford. H, S, CNOT and T are approximately universal up to global phase. Fault-tolerant resource estimates often count T gates and T depth because a logical T can require expensive magic-state resources in common error-correcting architectures. These are architecture-dependent costs, and syntactic T count may overestimate a simplified circuit.

</Depth>
