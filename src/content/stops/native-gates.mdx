<Depth level="kid">

Machines have their own small set of instructions. To use a new machine, rewrite your instructions using the ones it understands.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

For this lab imagine a device with RZ, SX and CZ. SX is a square root of X; two SX gates make X. A sequence of RZ, SX and RZ makes H up to global phase. A CZ with H before and after on its target makes CNOT.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

Here $SX=HSH$, so $SX^2=X$. $R_Z(\pi/2)SX R_Z(\pi/2)=e^{-i\pi/4}H$. Conjugating CZ by H on the target gives CX, and replacing those Hs gives the stated global phase. This basis is a mathematical example, not a claim about a particular currently available backend. For a real device consult its target instructions and calibration data.

</Depth>
