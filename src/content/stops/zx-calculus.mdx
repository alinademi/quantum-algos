<Depth level="kid">

Imagine replacing a long circuit drawing with dots and connecting lines. Each dot has a type and an angle written beside it. Two connected dots of the same type can sometimes become a single dot whose angle is their sum. A certain change of coordinates switches one type of dot to the other. Dots can also connect several wires; two such dots joined in a particular way describe a controlled flip. These rules make it possible to simplify a drawing without changing the calculation. The picture breaks because these are not ordinary diagrams: each dot represents a mathematical map, and only valid rewrite rules preserve that map.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

ZX diagrams use two kinds of spiders: Z and X. A one-input one-output Z spider is the phase gate P(α); an X spider is its version in the X basis. Two connected same-type spiders fuse by adding their angles. Applying H on all legs changes a spider's type. A multi-leg Z spider with zero angle copies standard-basis labels, and a multi-leg X spider similarly uses the X basis. Connect a Z copy to an X merge to obtain CNOT, with a normalization scalar. These [circuit identities](/learn/circuit-identities/) preserve the represented maps when their scalar conventions are respected, so they can simplify circuits, including [Clifford](/learn/clifford-group/) circuits.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

In the unnormalized convention a Z spider with m inputs and n outputs is $|0\rangle^{\otimes n}\langle0|^{\otimes m}+e^{i\alpha}|1\rangle^{\otimes n}\langle1|^{\otimes m}$. X spiders use |+⟩ and |−⟩ instead. Connected same-type spiders fuse by summing phases. H on every leg changes color. Joining a 1-to-2 Z spider on the control with a 2-to-1 X spider on the target gives CNOT divided by √2; multiplying by √2 restores its exact matrix. Scalar-free diagrams commonly omit that scalar, but quantitative probabilities require normalization. The examples test the independently defined maps against their rewrites. See [van de Wetering's review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13966).

</Depth>
