<Depth level="kid">

Imagine a puzzle with a temporary note that identifies which route a wave contribution came from. You can use the note to do something useful, but if the routes later join, leaving that distinguishing record behind can spoil their cancellation. Instead undo the instructions that wrote the note, after using the needed information. The workspace returns to its original state while the desired change remains. Now the contributions can combine correctly. In the lab, clearing the note makes the desired result certain, whereas leaving it reduces that chance. The picture breaks because the record is a quantum helper; simply throwing away a real note is not the same as reversing its creation.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

Compute a Boolean AND of two |+⟩ inputs into a clean [ancilla](/learn/ancillas/) q2 using Toffoli. CNOT into q3, prepared in |−⟩, changes the sign of the 11 contribution by kickback. Apply Toffoli again to clear q2, then CZ and H on both inputs. With q2 restored, the two signs cancel and both input wires end in 0 with certainty. Without the inverse, q2 remains entangled with the inputs, spoiling [interference](/learn/interference/): the chance of 00 is only 5/8. This is the quantum version of [reversible computing](/learn/reversible/): compute, use or copy the result, then undo the temporary work.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

Bennett's compute-copy-uncompute construction maps $|x,0,0\rangle$ to $|x,0,f(x)\rangle$ by compute $U_f$, XOR the output label, and apply $U_f^\dagger$. XOR copies orthogonal labels, not arbitrary unknown states. Here q3 starts in |−⟩ instead, so the copy step kicks back $(-1)^{ab}$. After uncomputation CZ cancels that phase and H on each data wire yields 00. Without cleanup the scratch retains the AND record, leaving the reduced input state mixed. After CZ and H, the probability of 00 is $(3/4)^2+(1/4)^2=5/8$, from the two orthogonal scratch records. Discarding the scratch cannot recover interference.

</Depth>
