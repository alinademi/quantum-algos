<Depth level="kid">

A gate can turn the arrow used to picture a qubit. The direction of the turn and its size are separate choices. Turn backward to undo it.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

Starting at 0, an X or Y rotation by 90 degrees produces equal measurement odds, but different complex amplitudes. A Z rotation changes the relative phase. An inverse rotation uses the negative angle.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

For a Pauli $P$, $R_P(\theta)=e^{-i\theta P/2}=\cos(\theta/2)I-i\sin(\theta/2)P$. Since $P^2=I$, this follows directly by separating even and odd terms in the exponential. $R_P(2\pi)=-I$, $R_P(4\pi)=I$. The rotation is right-handed in the usual Bloch coordinates, with $\hbar=1$ in the exponent convention.

</Depth>
