<Depth level="kid">

Imagine trying to turn a small globe from one position to another. You can turn it around a vertical line, then a horizontal line, then the vertical line again. With the right three angles you can reach any orientation. Some choices reach the same orientation in different ways.

An ideal qubit has a similar picture: three turns are enough to describe any single-qubit gate, apart from a common change to all its amplitude signs or timing that cannot be seen when the gate acts alone. The picture breaks because the globe has a literal orientation. The qubit has complex amplitudes, and its rotation angles describe a mathematical operation.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

Every one-qubit [unitary matrix](/learn/unitary-matrices/) can be written as a global phase times three [rotations](/learn/rotations/): first RZ(λ), then RY(θ), then RZ(φ). The angles describe an operation on every input, not only one starting qubit.

A compact gate called U(θ,φ,λ) in one common convention produces the same result up to global phase. Both describe arbitrary one-qubit operations once an overall phase is allowed. Choose three angles in the lab, inspect each step and compare the Euler product and U matrices. The direct matrices can differ by a common factor even though all measurements of an unconditional gate agree. Relative phase, unlike global phase, is observable.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

A common convention (OpenQASM 3 and Qiskit) is $U(\theta,\phi,\lambda)=e^{i(\phi+\lambda)/2}R_Z(\phi)R_Y(\theta)R_Z(\lambda)$. Its first column is $(\cos(\theta/2),e^{i\phi}\sin(\theta/2))^T$; its second is $(-e^{i\lambda}\sin(\theta/2),e^{i(\phi+\lambda)}\cos(\theta/2))^T$.

OpenQASM 2 uses the Euler product without the prefactor (Cross et al., eq. 2); the conventions differ only by a global phase. Chronological order is rightmost matrix first. These rotations cover SU(2), and multiplying by a phase covers U(2). The [phase gate](/learn/phase-gates/) and RZ conventions likewise differ by a global phase. Do not discard phases when making controlled versions without checking the relative phase between control branches.

</Depth>
