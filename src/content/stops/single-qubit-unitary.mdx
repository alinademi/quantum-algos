<Depth level="kid">

You can point an arrow in a new direction by making a few simple turns. A qubit gate can likewise be made from three turns, with an overall phase.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

A general one-qubit gate can use a Z turn, then a Y turn, then another Z turn. Two gates that differ only by a global phase act the same when used alone. The same three angles work for every input state, not just 0.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

One common convention is $U(\theta,\phi,\lambda)=e^{i(\phi+\lambda)/2}R_Z(\phi)R_Y(\theta)R_Z(\lambda)$. Its first column is $(\cos(\theta/2),e^{i\phi}\sin(\theta/2))^T$ and its second is $(-e^{i\lambda}\sin(\theta/2),e^{i(\phi+\lambda)}\cos(\theta/2))^T$. This covers U(2) up to an additional global phase; the chronological order starts with $R_Z(\lambda)$.

</Depth>
