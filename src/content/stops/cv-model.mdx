<Depth level="kid">

Instead of an arrow with two amplitudes, picture the position and momentum of a quantum oscillator. You can shift, rotate and squeeze its uncertainty pattern.

</Depth>

<Depth level="student">

A vacuum mode has equal uncertainty in both quadratures. Squeezing reduces one variance while increasing the other. Displacing moves the center without changing variances; a rotation turns both the center and uncertainty ellipse.

</Depth>

<Depth level="engineer">

Use dimensionless quadratures with $[x,p]=i$, vacuum covariance $I/2$, and symmetrized covariance $V_{jk}=\langle\{R_j-\bar R_j,R_k-\bar R_k\}\rangle/2$. The pure squeezed vacuum has variances $e^{-2r}/2$ and $e^{2r}/2$. A rotation O maps $V\mapsto OVO^T$ and the displacement similarly. $\det V=1/4$ for this pure Gaussian family, and $\Delta x\Delta p\ge1/2$. Gaussian preparations, operations and Gaussian measurements alone admit efficient classical descriptions; universal CV computation under standard gate-set constructions requires non-Gaussian resources. See [Lloyd and Braunstein](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9810082).

</Depth>
